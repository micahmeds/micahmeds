The Problem

Help exists. It just cannot get there in time.

Our 911 system was built decades ago. It leaves communities waiting 7 to 14 minutes for help that — in cardiac arrest, stroke, overdose, or severe trauma — is needed within 4 to 6. In historically underserved neighborhoods, that wait runs longer still.

EMS must navigate distance, traffic, call volume, and staffing shortages, often on technology built long before the real-time mobile era. Meanwhile a qualified responder may already be nearby — with no reliable way to know that help is needed.

How MicahMeds Solves It

The algorithm sends whoever can actually get there first.

1
An emergency is reported
Through the MicahMeds app or a 911 dispatcher referral. Location and condition data are captured immediately.
2
The triage engine compares who is closest
Location, availability, credentials, scope of practice, and estimated arrival time are compared across nearby EMS units, licensed healthcare professionals, and certified MicahMeds responders.
3
The responder arrives, then hands off to EMS
Alongside the 911 dispatch, never in place of it — followed by a structured handoff when EMS arrives. EMS remains the default for critical and life-threatening calls.
Who responds: off-duty nurses, physicians, PAs, and paramedics already driving nearby — plus certified NEMTAC and EMT-Basic responders trained in their own communities.

Why Now

Baltimore's dispatch backbone is twenty years old

At an April 2026 City Council investigative hearing, Baltimore's chief information officer testified that the twenty-year-old computer-aided dispatch system linking police, fire, and 911 will be replaced — and that the work will likely take several years.

Roughly $12 million in state and federal funding has been secured. Vendor procurement is only now beginning. A comparable replacement in Denver took four years.

Council President Zeke Cohen described the system as what lets the city send the right responders to the right place at the right time.

The fix is coming.
It is also years away.

MicahMeds is designed as a parallel path that does not depend on a single legacy system — so that during those years, a qualified responder already nearby can still be reached.

Source: Baltimore City Council investigative hearing, April 2026, as reported by WJZ / CBS News Baltimore. The same official testified the system had not failed during 2026.

The Founder

The idea started in front of a television. A City Council hearing was on the news — the city's dispatch system, two decades old, a replacement still years out. I had spent a decade treating people whose recoveries were shaped by minutes that passed before anyone reached them. Sitting there, the two things connected: the system will be fixed eventually, and in the meantime, someone qualified is almost always already close by.

More than a decade in healthcare — an associate degree, a bachelor's in Allied Health and Healthcare Management, and a master's degree — alongside years of direct patient care as an occupational therapist and physical therapist assistant.

That work built a foundation in clinical decision-making, therapeutic intervention, and recovery. It also made clear that the impact could reach further than one patient at a time. MicahMeds is grounded in clinical and community understanding rather than logistics alone.

Become a Responder

A workforce program, not a gig.

MicahMeds is developing a certification program for people who already drive — rideshare, delivery, and commercial drivers making a living behind the wheel — turning time on the road into a credential, a career pathway, and a way to help in their own neighborhoods. A separate pathway for already-licensed healthcare professionals is in development, pending legal and clinical review.

Responders operate as licensed independent contractors: they own their vehicles, set their own hours, and accept or decline each dispatch. All complete background screening and vehicle inspection, and carry minimum liability coverage, under medical director oversight and documented protocols.

🩺
Tier P · Credentialed ProfessionalsIn Development

A pathway being designed for nurses, physicians, PAs, and paramedics.

Thousands of licensed clinicians drive rideshare and delivery between shifts, on days off, and throughout residency. They are already on the road, already trained to assess and stabilize, and frequently within blocks of an emergency they are qualified to manage — with no mechanism that permits them to respond.

Tier P is the pathway being designed for them: a route for off-duty clinicians to respond within their existing scope of practice. The liability, Good Samaritan, and medical-direction framework is currently under review with healthcare counsel and state EMS authorities, and no Tier P responders will be onboarded until that review is complete.

The MicahMeds Certification Program

Already driving? We will train you.

No clinical background required. The MicahMeds Certification Program is being developed to create structured training and workforce-development pathways for professional drivers and community members — so that the hours you already spend on the road become a credential that belongs to you, wherever you go next.

Start here

Tier 1 — NEMTAC

Forty hours of training plus First Aid and CPR. The entry point for any driver, with no prior medical experience needed.

Scope of response
Falls · Minor bleeding · Diabetic events · Panic episodes · Minor burns
Advance to

Tier 2 — EMT-Basic

120 to 150 hours to a nationally recognized EMT-Basic certification — a credential that opens doors across the entire healthcare field.

Scope of response
Cardiac arrest · Respiratory distress · Severe bleeding · Anaphylaxis · Overdose · Stroke · Severe trauma
Grow into

Tier 3 — EMR / Paramedic

1,000+ hours at EMR or Paramedic level. A full clinical career, reached one tier at a time. Opening as the network matures.

Scope of response
Advanced cardiac life support · Advanced airway · Complex trauma management
The ladder is the point. A driver can enter at Tier 1 with no medical background and progress toward a full clinical career — each credential nationally recognized and permanently theirs, whether they stay with MicahMeds or not.
